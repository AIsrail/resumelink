You apply. You hear nothing. Not a rejection, not an auto-reply beyond the first one — just silence. If that's been happening to you a lot lately, the data says you're not imagining it.

## The numbers are getting worse, not better

According to Criteria Corp's 2026 Candidate Experience Report, **53% of job seekers say an employer ghosted them in the past year** — up from 38% in 2024. That's a 15-point jump in two years. Instant Interview's independent survey landed on the same 53% figure.

It's not one-sided, either. Greenhouse's 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report found that **one in two candidates has ghosted an employer back** — missing a scheduled interview or going silent after an offer. Ghosting has become a two-way norm, not a rude exception.

## Why employers are doing it

Resume Genius surveyed hiring managers directly in 2026 and asked why *they* ghost candidates. The top reasons:

- **Indecision internally** — 86%
- **Too many applicants to respond to individually** — 86%
- **Candidate's salary expectations above budget** — 82%

Notice what's missing from that list: your resume formatting, your choice of font, whether you used a template. The top reasons are almost entirely about the employer's internal process, not about you.

## A big piece of the puzzle: ghost jobs

Here's a statistic that reframes the whole problem. Depending on the source, somewhere between **25% and 38% of active online job listings in 2026 are "ghost jobs"** — postings for roles that aren't being actively filled. Companies post them to:

- Build a talent pipeline for later
- Signal growth to investors or the market
- Keep an existing employee aware they're "replaceable"
- Comply with internal policy requiring external posting even when an internal hire is already planned

If a third of the jobs you're applying to were never going to produce a reply, that changes how you should read the silence. It's not a judgment on your application — a lot of the time, there was no real process to respond to.

## What this means for your job search

**1. Stop reading silence as rejection.** Given the ghost job rate, a non-response is genuinely ambiguous. It could be a hiring freeze, a filled role, or budget cuts — not something you did wrong in your application.

**2. Apply to more roles than feels "efficient."** If a meaningful share of listings are dead ends through no fault of your own, volume becomes part of the strategy, not a sign of a weak resume.

**3. Prioritize warm paths over cold applications.** Referrals and direct outreach don't go through the same ghost-job noise — see our [companion post on actual hiring odds](/blog/real-odds-job-search-2026/) for why referrals specifically change your odds.

**4. Don't over-optimize your resume chasing a theory.** If the top reasons for silence are internal (budget, indecision, volume), spending another three hours tweaking your resume's bullet points has a lower ceiling than you'd think. Get it solid, make sure it's ATS-readable, then spend the saved time applying to more places or reaching out directly to someone at the company.

## The takeaway

Ghosting rates are rising on both sides, a large share of listings may not be real openings, and the top reasons employers give for silence are about their own process — not your application. That doesn't make the silence less frustrating. But it does mean you can stop re-writing your resume for the fifth time hoping it'll fix a problem that was never about the resume.
